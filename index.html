The ArgentineLifecycle.

Begin with the honeymoon. Buenos Aires on a spring evening, a long lunch under the Andes in a Mendoza vineyard, a lake in Patagonia with nobody else on it. Argentina is the easy third place for the months you are not in the United States or the Emirates: visa-free on both your passports, a dollar that still goes a long way, and a winter that falls exactly in the Gulf's hottest months.

If the honeymoon does not want to end, there are paths to a longer stay, to residency, and in time to Argentine citizenship for both of you and for any child born here. This one document holds the whole arc, from the first ninety days to a passport and a business, checked against Argentine law and prices as they stand this month, and honest about the parts that are still moving.

The honeymoon

Start with the good part

Before any office, a month or two of the country at its best. The pictures below are the four regions the rest of the guide compares, plus the two trips everyone makes from Buenos Aires: the falls at Iguazú and the coloured hills of the north-west. Nothing on this page needs a visa, a lawyer or a form. It needs the two of you and the right season.

When to come

From the Emirates

The Gulf's worst months are Argentina's best excuse. From June to September, when Dubai sits above 40 degrees, Buenos Aires is a crisp 12 to 16 degrees with clear skies, Mendoza and the north-west are dry and sunny, and Bariloche has snow on Cerro Catedral. The Gulf's pleasant winter, November to March, is Argentine summer: long Patagonian days, the Atlantic coast, and the grape harvest in Mendoza.

From the States

Buenos Aires is one to two hours ahead of New York, so there is no jet lag, only a long dinner. Nonstop flights from New York, Miami, Atlanta, Houston and Dallas take ten to eleven hours overnight. Thanksgiving and Christmas fall in early summer here; the Vendimia harvest festival fills Mendoza in the first week of March.

The seasons, reversed

December to February: summer. Patagonia, the lakes, the coast; Buenos Aires is hot and half-empty in January. March to May: autumn, the harvest, golden vineyards, the best months in the capital. June to August: winter, skiing at Bariloche, mild days in Buenos Aires, dry sun in Salta and Jujuy. September to November: spring, jacarandas in November, whales off Península Valdés.

Costs are the pleasant surprise. A comfortable honeymoon month for two runs about USD 250 to 400 a day on the ground, flights excluded, with dinner at a good parrilla under USD 40 a head and a bottle of serious Malbec at the table for USD 15 to 25. The lodges of the Uco Valley and the lakefront hotels of Bariloche are the exception, at USD 500 to 1,200 a night, and worth two or three nights each rather than a week.

Practicalities are few. Both passports enter without a visa. Foreign cards now settle at the official rate, so there is no need to carry bricks of dollars, though a few hundred in cash still opens doors outside the cities. Domestic flights are cheap and frequent from Aeroparque, the city airport. Argentine Spanish is warm, fast and forgiving of visitors who try.

If it does not want to end

The ninety days on the stamp can become one hundred and eighty with a single extension at the migration office. A temporary residence, for remote workers, people with foreign income, employees or investors, turns visitors into residents and starts the clock the citizenship rules count. A child born here is Argentine at birth and gives both parents the right to permanent residence. And after two years of continuous legal residence, or sooner if the courts' old shortcuts stand, either of you can ask a judge for citizenship and a passport that opens 169 destinations. Phases I to IV walk that road step by step; Phase V is for the business you might build along it.

Overview

How to read this guide, and what has changed since the source was written

The honeymoon chapter above is the pleasant part; this one is the map of everything after it. The lifecycle runs in five phases. Each phase has a chapter with the rules, the costs, the paperwork and a figure or two. The knowledge base at the end holds the glossary, the directory of offices and hospitals, the questions people ask, and a risk register. The planner turns it into a budget, a checklist and the eight decisions only the two of you can make.

The legal ground moved twice since the source guide was written

The source guide describes citizenship as a judicial right, granted by a federal judge, with a fast track for the parents of an Argentine-born child. That was the law for over a century. Then two things happened.

First, on 29 May 2025 the government issued Decree 366/2025, a decree of necessity and urgency (emergency law by the president). It moved naturalisation from the courts to the National Migration Directorate, the DNM (the migration office), required two years of continuous legal residence with no trips abroad, and struck out the old shortcuts, including the one for parents of Argentine children. It also introduced a citizenship-by-investment route that has never been given a price. The DNM began taking digital applications on 6 October 2025.

Second, on 30 June 2026 the National Electoral Chamber (the top electoral court) declared that decree absolutely null in the case of Liping Yang, a Chinese shopkeeper in Entre Ríos. The court held that citizenship is electoral matter, which the Constitution forbids the president to change by decree, and ordered every federal judge in the country to resume granting citizenship. The next day the government said it would appeal to the Supreme Court. As of the date of this guide no Supreme Court ruling has been reported.

So there are two possible worlds, and this guide plans for both. Scenario A: the ruling stands, citizenship is judicial again, and the old rules with their shortcuts apply. Scenario B: the Supreme Court reinstates the decree, and the two-year clock with no exits governs. The practical advice that survives both scenarios is the same: get legal residence early, keep it unbroken, and document a real life here.

I Phase I. Days 0 to 90

Arrive, explore, and start the paper trail

The tourist window is a low-stakes test of the country. Use it to choose a city, meet the doctors and the lawyer, and gather the documents that every later step will ask for. Nothing you do in these ninety days commits you, but the habits you form here decide how easy the next two years are.

Status: visitor (residente transitorio)Key office: none yetKey deadline: day 80

Entry: what the border asks for

Both passports

  • No visa. US and South African citizens are admitted for up to 90 days for tourism and other unpaid activities. No reciprocity fee applies.
  • A passport with six months left. Argentina itself only requires it to be valid, but airlines and neighbouring countries use the six-month rule, so keep to it.
  • Travel health insurance for the whole stay. Decree 366/2025 made it a condition of entry; whether it is still enforced after the June 2026 ruling is unclear. Have it regardless.
  • Proof of onward travel and of funds may be requested. A refundable ticket out and a bank statement on your phone are enough.
  • Cash above USD 10,000 per person must be declared to customs on arrival.

What officers now look for

Since the 2025 investigations into paid birth-tourism networks, officers at Ezeiza have been questioning some arrivals about their intent to reside. Certain nationalities get more of this than others; US and South African passports are not a focus. The rule is simple: answer as a visitor because you are one. You are here to explore, you have a return ticket, you have insurance. Do not arrive with a folder of residency papers. Do not say you have come to have a baby. Both are lawful intentions, but the tourist stamp is for tourists, and the honest and correct way to change status is inside the country, at the DNM, as Phase II explains.

The ninety days, as a plan

Weeks 1 to 2: land and orient

  • Rent a furnished flat for a month in the district you are most likely to choose. In Buenos Aires that means Palermo, Recoleta or Belgrano.
  • Get an Argentine SIM (Personal, Movistar or Claro) and a SUBE card for buses and the metro.
  • Open the two apps the country runs on: Mercado Pago and WhatsApp. Pay in pesos by card; foreign cards settle near the official rate now that the currency market is unified.
  • Meet one immigration lawyer and one accountant. Ask both what they have seen since the 30 June 2026 ruling.

Weeks 3 to 8: compare and decide

  • Spend a week each in your two runner-up regions. Fly Aerolíneas or Flybondi to Mendoza and Córdoba; Bariloche is a two-hour flight.
  • Tour the hospitals in Phase III. Ask each for its self-pay maternity package in writing.
  • Request quotes from two prepagas (private health plans) for two adults.
  • Start a Spanish tutor. Twice a week is enough to make the paperwork, and later the judge, easier.
  • Order the home-country documents listed in the checklist below. They are the long pole in the tent.

Weeks 9 to 12: commit and file

  • Sign a lease in your own names, ideally for 12 months. Real estate contracts have been freely negotiable since 2024; many landlords quote in dollars and accept a caution insurance policy instead of a local guarantor.
  • Choose your Phase II route by day 80 and act on it in the last ten days before your stamp expires.
  • If you are staying, open the Radex file for a temporary residence category and get the precaria (provisional residence certificate). From that moment you are legally resident, not visiting.

Start the paper trail on day one

The source guide calls the first ninety days "the evidentiary foundation". It is right, and the foundation has a shape. Every later authority, the DNM, a prepaga, a bank, a judge, will ask you to prove that you live here rather than visit. Evidence accumulates slowly, so begin at once: a lease in your names, utility bills, a local doctor, a Spanish tutor's invoices, membership of a club or a church. Keep every stamped page and every receipt in one folder, physical and scanned.

Two documents can only come from home, and both are slow. The US partner needs an FBI Identity History Summary, apostilled by the US Department of State; allow four to eight weeks. The South African partner needs a SAPS Police Clearance Certificate, apostilled by DIRCO; allow three to six months, and order it before you fly if you can. Both partners need apostilled birth certificates. Every foreign document must then be translated in Argentina by a traductor público (sworn translator) and legalised at the translators' college. Budget USD 800 to 1,500 for the set.

Three things not to do as a tourist

Do not take paid work for an Argentine employer. Remote work for a foreign employer is tolerated and there is a permit that formalises it (Phase II).

Do not overstay. Even a day past the stamp costs a fee to leave and creates a record that a judge can later read as disregard.

Do not pay anyone who promises a "citizenship package". The 2025 prosecutions were aimed at exactly those intermediaries, and their clients' papers were questioned along with them.

II Phase II. Days 90 to 180 and beyond

Keep your status unbroken

Continuous legal status is the thread every later step hangs from. Under the old rules a judge weighed it; under the 2025 decree it is counted in months and broken by a single trip abroad. Whichever regime wins in the Supreme Court, the safe play is the same: never let a day go undocumented, and move from visitor to resident as early as your paperwork allows.

Status: visitor, then residentKey office: DNM, Av. Antártida Argentina 1355, RetiroKey window: the 10 days before your stamp expires

Why "irregular" is the word to avoid

The source guide cites Fallos 332:1466, the Supreme Court's 2009 decision in Ni, I-Hsing, which held that an irregular migration status does not by itself bar naturalisation. That precedent is real, and it is back in play now that the courts are deciding citizenship again. But it was a rescue for people who fell through the cracks, not a plan. Judges since then have leaned on legal residence as the clearest evidence of the two years the Constitution requires, and the 2025 decree, if reinstated, makes legal residence a hard condition. Treat a gap in status as something you would have to explain to a judge under oath, because that is exactly what it becomes.

Day 80: three doors

Door one: the DNM extension

A tourist stay of three months can be extended once for a similar period. You apply at the DNM in Retiro (mornings, 8 to 13), or at the delegations in Mendoza, Córdoba and other cities, within the ten days before the stamp expires. Bring the passport and pay the renewal fee. If you turn up in the thirty days after expiry the fee carries a 50% surcharge; after that, no extension is possible and you must buy an exit permit (habilitación de salida) to leave.

The extension is the cleanest paper trail a visitor can have: a DNM record that you asked permission and were granted it. It is the right choice if you have not yet chosen a city or are waiting for home-country certificates.

Door two: leave and come back

Colonia del Sacramento is seventy-five minutes from Buenos Aires by ferry; Santiago is a day's drive from Mendoza. A new entry stamp resets the ninety days. This works, and thousands do it, but it is a de facto reset, not a legal category. Officers keep the record, and after the 2025 tightening a pattern of resets invites questions about intent. The decree also made "residence" turn on legal category, not on stamps, so resets buy days without building anything. Use them for a real trip, not as a strategy.

Door three: change category

Argentina lets a visitor apply from inside the country for a temporary residence in a category that fits. The file is opened online on Radex (the DNM's application portal), a fee is paid, and while the DNM decides you hold a precaria, a provisional certificate that lets you stay, work and study. The categories that fit a couple like you are in the table below. This is the door the source guide's "Stage 2 transition" really means, and it is the only one that counts under the decree.

What the Radex file needs, and how long it takes

The file

  • Passport and the entry stamp or digital entry record.
  • Apostilled and translated birth certificate.
  • Police certificates from every country you lived in for a year or more during the last three years, apostilled and translated. This is where the FBI and SAPS documents go.
  • Argentine criminal record certificate from the Registro Nacional de Reincidencia, obtained online in a day or two.
  • Proof of address: the lease, a utility bill, or a certificate from the police station.
  • The category proof: income, contract, enrolment or the child's certificate.
  • The DNM fee, paid online. A sworn statement of no criminal record and a health declaration.

The sequence

  • Upload everything on Radex; the system issues an appointment and the precaria, usually within days.
  • Attend the DNM in person for fingerprints and a photo.
  • Approval takes weeks to a few months depending on category and office load. The DNI arrives by post afterwards.
  • With the DNI you can get a CUIL or CUIT (tax identification numbers), open a full bank account, sign up for a prepaga at the standard rate, and register as a monotributista (small taxpayer regime) if you invoice locally.
  • Keep the precaria current if the decision drags; it is renewable.
If you end up in Scenario B

If the Supreme Court reinstates the decree, the two years that count are two years of legal residence with no exit from Argentina, immediately before you apply. Officials said exceptions would be written into regulations, but as of mid-2026 the DNM system simply rejected applicants who had crossed the border. So once you hold a temporary or permanent residence, treat every foreign trip as a decision to restart the clock, until the rules say otherwise. That includes weekend trips to Uruguay.

III Phase III. Months 4 to 12

Healthcare, and a birth that changes everyone's status

Argentina's private hospitals are among the best in the region and a child born on Argentine soil is Argentine from the first breath. The source guide is right about both. What it undersells is timing: the cheapest and safest way to have a baby here is to be inside a private plan before the pregnancy begins, and the residency the birth unlocks for the parents now comes with conditions.

Status: resident, then parents of a citizenKey offices: Registro Civil, RENAPER, DNMKey rule: join a prepaga before conception

How care is paid for

Public hospitals

Free at the point of use and often excellent, especially in the capital. The 2025 decree let provinces charge non-residents for non-urgent care and some do; emergencies are always treated. A Buenos Aires public maternity such as the Sardá is a serious institution, but you would share a ward and wait. Keep it as the safety net it is.

Prepagas

Private health plans (monthly fee, private network). The big names are OSDE, Swiss Medical, Galeno, Medicus and the hospitals' own plans: the Hospital Italiano's, the Alemán's, the Británico's. Every plan must cover the PMO, the compulsory medical programme, which includes the Plan Materno Infantil: pregnancy, birth and the baby's first year with no copayments. Plans differ in the hospitals you may use, the rooms, and the doctors.

Self-pay

Every private hospital sells a maternity package to patients without cover: obstetric fees, anaesthesia, theatre, a private room for two or three nights. It is the source guide's recommendation because it sidesteps waiting periods. It is the right call if the pregnancy is already under way when you arrive. It leaves you exposed to the expensive complications, which is the point of the figure below.

The waiting-period trap, explained

By law a prepaga cannot impose waiting periods (carencias) on PMO services, and pregnancy care is PMO. What it can do is treat a pregnancy that already exists when you sign up as a pre-existing condition (preexistencia): it may charge a higher monthly fee for it, or in practice steer you to a costlier plan. Join while you are not yet pregnant and the whole pregnancy is covered at the standard price. That single sequencing decision is worth several thousand dollars.

The pregnancy, the Argentine way

Before the birth

  • Choose an obstetrician (obstetra) who delivers at your chosen hospital; in Argentina the doctor, not the hospital, usually attends the birth, and many work with a midwife (partera).
  • Monthly check-ups, three routine scans, the standard blood panels. All PMO if you are in a plan.
  • Pre-register at the hospital's maternity around week 30. Ask about the room, the companion policy, and the neonatal unit.
  • Prenatal classes (curso de preparto) are common and a good place to meet local parents.
  • The C-section rate in private hospitals is high, above half of births in some. If you want a natural birth, say so early and choose the team accordingly.

After the birth

  • The hospital issues the medical birth certificate (certificado de nacido vivo). In the capital many hospitals have a Registro Civil desk; otherwise register within 40 days.
  • The baby's DNI is often issued at the hospital and arrives within weeks. The Argentine passport follows at RENAPER; both parents sign.
  • Paediatrics: the first-year schedule of visits and the national vaccine calendar, free at public vaccination points and covered by any plan.
  • Register the birth with your own countries: a Consular Report of Birth Abroad at the US Embassy in Palermo, and a registration of birth abroad through the South African Embassy. Both take weeks; start within the first month.

What the birth does for the parents

The child is Argentine by birth under article 1 of Law 346, with the sole exception of the children of foreign diplomats. That has not changed and cannot change by decree; it is in the Constitution's spirit and in a law from 1869. Under the migration law both parents of an Argentine citizen may apply for permanent residence at once, without first holding a temporary category. The decree kept this right and added two conditions: you must show sufficient means to live here and you must have no disqualifying criminal record. Both are easy for you to meet, and both are things a judge would have looked at anyway.

What the birth no longer does automatically is shorten the road to citizenship. That depends on which legal world you are in when you file, and Phase IV lays out both. The honest summary: permanent residence for the parents is fast and secure; citizenship is somewhere between one and three years behind it.

Two home-country notes. The US parent's child can be a US citizen from birth if that parent has lived in the United States long enough, generally five years with two of them after age fourteen; the rules differ for unmarried parents, and the embassy's checklist is the authority. The South African parent's child is a South African citizen by birth once the birth is registered with Home Affairs through the embassy. Argentina does not ask either of you to give anything up: an Argentine by naturalisation keeps every other nationality, and both the United States and, since the Constitutional Court's ruling of May 2025, South Africa allow it.

On birth tourism and the law

Having a child in Argentina and claiming the rights that follow is lawful. What prosecutors pursued in 2025 was fraud: intermediaries charging USD 6,500 to 40,000 for fake addresses, forged residence papers and bulk passport applications, mostly for clients who left the day the passport arrived. The distinction the authorities draw is genuine intent to reside: a real home, a real doctor, a tax footprint, time actually spent here. Everything in Phases I and II builds that record. Nothing in this plan requires an intermediary, and you should refuse any who offer.

IV Phase IV. Months 12 to 36

Citizenship: two possible worlds, one preparation

Argentine naturalisation was a judicial act from 1869 until October 2025, an administrative one for nine months, and a judicial one again since 30 June 2026, pending the Supreme Court. The law you will file under is not knowable today. The file you need to build is.

Status: permanent resident, then citizenKey body: federal court with electoral competence, or the DNMKey law: Ley 346, Decreto 3213/84, Decreto 366/2025

The architecture, in five documents

The Constitution, article 20

Foreigners enjoy the civil rights of citizens and may naturalise after two years of continuous residence, a term the authority "may shorten" for those who prove services to the Republic. Every later rule is an interpretation of this sentence.

Law 346 of 1869

Article 1 makes everyone born on Argentine soil Argentine. Article 2 sets naturalisation at two years of residence declared before a federal judge, and lists the services that shorten it. It has been amended many times and never replaced.

Decree 3213/84

The regulation that courts applied for forty years. Its article 3 lists the cases where the two years are not required: having an Argentine spouse or child, holding public office, serving in the forces, teaching, settling Tierra del Fuego, founding an industry, among others. This is the source guide's "fast track".

Decree 366/2025 and Decree 524/2025

The May 2025 emergency decree rewrote Law 346: two years of continuous legal residence with no exit from the country, the DNM as the deciding body, no shortcuts except a "relevant investment". Decree 524/2025 of 31 July 2025 wrote the procedure for the investment route and left the amount to the Ministry of Economy, which had not published one by March 2026.

The National Electoral Chamber, 30 June 2026

In Yang, Liping s/ nacionalidad y ciudadanía the Chamber declared Decree 366/2025 absolutely null: citizenship confers political rights, political and electoral matter is barred to emergency decrees by article 99(3) of the Constitution, and there was no emergency. It granted Mr Yang citizenship, told the Security Ministry to instruct the DNM, and notified every federal electoral judge. The government announced an appeal to the Supreme Court on 1 July 2026.

What the judge, or the file, will ask for

What citizenship changes

  • An Argentine passport and DNI, and the right to live and work across Mercosur.
  • Voting is compulsory for citizens aged 18 to 70, naturalised included.
  • No military service; it has been voluntary since 1994.
  • Citizenship gained by fraud can be annulled. Citizenship gained honestly cannot be lost.

What it costs

  • Court filing is free; the state does not charge for the carta de ciudadanía.
  • Lawyer's fees run USD 2,000 to 6,000 per adult (indicative); fixed-fee engagements are normal.
  • Fresh certificates, translations and legalisations, USD 300 to 800.
  • Passport fees at RENAPER are modest; express service costs more.

Your other passports

  • The United States allows dual nationality and taxes you either way.
  • South Africa's Constitutional Court struck down automatic loss of citizenship on 6 May 2025; no retention letter is needed any more.
  • Argentina never asks for renunciation; a naturalised Argentine keeps all prior nationalities.
A quiet asymmetry worth naming

The passport is a big prize for one of you and a nice-to-have for the other. The child's Argentine citizenship is a certainty for both. If the Supreme Court makes Scenario B the law, it is worth asking whether both parents need to run the two-year no-exit clock at the same time, or whether one holds the family's freedom to travel while the other naturalises first. Nothing in the law requires you to file together.

The set-up sequence

  1. Each foreign owner obtains a CDI or, with a DNI, a CUIT from ARCA (the tax agency, formerly AFIP).
  2. Choose the form and the seat: the IGJ in the capital, or the provincial registry in Córdoba or Mendoza.
  3. Register, obtain the company's CUIT, and open the bank account; banks will ask for the shareholders' identities and source of funds.
  4. Register as an employer and take out workers' compensation insurance (ART) before the first hire.
  5. If you will sponsor foreign staff, enrol the company in the DNM's employer register (RENURE).
  6. If most of the revenue is software or services exported, apply to the Knowledge Economy regime.

Incentives that apply to a firm your size

  • Knowledge Economy Law (27.506). Registered companies whose activity is mostly software, IT services, R&D or professional exports get a reduced corporate income tax rate and a tax credit against employer contributions. It is the one incentive built for a nearshoring firm.
  • Exports of services are outside VAT. Check the current rules on bringing the dollars in; the obligation to convert export proceeds eased in 2025 and keeps changing.
  • RIGI, the large-investment regime, needs USD 200 million and is not for you.
  • Tierra del Fuego (Law 19.640) exempts activity located in the province from VAT and income tax; real only if the team really sits in Ushuaia.

Hiring under the March 2026 law

Law 27.802, the Labour Modernisation Law, was passed on 27 February 2026 and published on 6 March. A court briefly suspended eighty of its articles; the Labour Appeals Chamber lifted that injunction in April and the law is in force. For a small employer the changes that matter are these. The trial period is now six months, or up to eight in firms with five staff or fewer, during which either side can end the contract without severance. Severance remains one month per year of service, but the base excludes the aguinaldo, holiday pay and non-monthly bonuses, and is capped at three times the average wage of the collective agreement with a floor of 67% of actual pay. Collective agreements may replace severance with an employer-funded severance fund. Union dues deducted from pay are capped at 2%. Company benefits such as meals, health plans and phones no longer count as salary for severance.

Contractors on monotributo remain common for remote roles and are lawful when the relationship is genuinely independent; a full-time, exclusive, directed contractor is an employee in disguise and a court will say so. Pay employees in pesos through a payroll provider; the aguinaldo, a thirteenth salary paid in two halves in June and December, is mandatory, as are fourteen days' paid holiday in year one, rising with tenure.

The tax rule that arrives at month twelve

Argentina

A foreign national becomes an Argentine tax resident on obtaining permanent residence, or after twelve months of presence in the country; short absences do not reset the count. From then on Argentina taxes your worldwide income at rates up to 35% and your worldwide net assets under the personal assets tax, whose rates were cut in 2024 and fall further through 2027. Companies pay income tax on a sliding scale from 25% to 35%, VAT at 21% on domestic sales, and provincial turnover tax. There is no income tax treaty between Argentina and the United States, only an information-sharing agreement, so double-tax relief runs through each country's unilateral credits.

Home

The US partner keeps filing US returns and foreign account reports every year; citizenship-based taxation does not end with a move, and the foreign earned income exclusion and foreign tax credits are the tools that keep it bearable. The South African partner should decide deliberately whether to cease South African tax residency, which is a formal SARS process with a deemed disposal of assets, rather than let it drift. Both of you should sit with a cross-border adviser before month ten, when the choices are still open.

The nearshoring pitch, updated

Sell Argentina to North American clients on what is still true: senior engineers and analysts with strong English, a same-day working overlap with every US time zone, a labour law that is now easier to work under, and a cost that is roughly 40% below US rates for equivalent seniority. Do not sell it on a cheap currency; that argument dated in 2024 and a client who checks will notice.

Questions couples ask

Do we need a visa?Entry

No. Both US and South African citizens are admitted for up to ninety days without a visa. Carry insurance, a return or onward ticket, and proof of funds. There is no reciprocity fee.

Can we work while we are on the tourist stamp?Entry

Not for an Argentine employer or client. Working remotely for foreign clients is tolerated in practice and the digital nomad permit exists to make it formal. Once you hold a precaria you may work for anyone.

Can we open a bank account as visitors?Money

Rarely; banks want a DNI and a CUIT or CUIL. Until then use foreign cards, which now settle near the official rate, Western Union for pesos in cash, and dollar cash for deposits and rent. A CDI lets you buy property without a DNI. With the DNI, a full account and Mercado Pago open up within days.

Do we need to be married?Family

Not for the child route: both biological parents of an Argentine are entitled to permanent residence, married or not. Marriage matters if one of you wants residence as the spouse of the other, and for inheritance and some tax questions. Civil marriage in Argentina is open to foreigners with a few weeks' notice and translated documents.

Is the baby automatically Argentine?Family

Yes. Everyone born on Argentine soil is Argentine, the children of foreign diplomats excepted. The DNI and passport follow the birth registration. The baby is also entitled to US citizenship if the US parent meets the physical-presence rule, and to South African citizenship on registration of the birth abroad.

Is having a baby here to gain citizenship legal?Law

Birthright citizenship is the law and claiming the rights that follow is lawful. The 2025 prosecutions targeted fraud: paid schemes, false addresses, forged residence papers. Genuine residence with a documented life is both the honest path and the defence.

How long until we hold Argentine passports?Law

Under Scenario A, about twelve to twenty-four months after the birth. Under Scenario B, about thirty to forty months, most of it the two-year no-exit clock. No timeline is guaranteed; court backlogs and document delays are the usual causes of slippage.

What if the Supreme Court rules while we are mid-process?Law

Files already lodged in court under Scenario A would most likely continue, since courts apply the law in force when they rule; a lawyer should say so in writing. The practical hedge is to file in court as early as the ruling allows, and to avoid foreign trips once you hold residence in case the clock is reinstated.

Will we pay Argentine tax?Money

After twelve months of presence, or on permanent residence, yes: worldwide income and worldwide assets. The US partner keeps US obligations regardless. The South African partner should formally decide about SARS residency. There is no US-Argentina tax treaty. Get advice before month ten.

How safe is it?Life

Buenos Aires is a big city: phone snatching and pickpocketing are the common crimes, violent crime against foreigners is rare in the districts named here. Mendoza and Córdoba are calmer; Patagonian towns are very safe. Use taxis or apps at night, keep phones out of sight on the street.

How much Spanish do we need?Life

Enough to follow a doctor and a clerk. Rioplatense Spanish uses vos and a soft "sh" for ll and y; it is easy to understand once your ear adjusts. Judges expect basic conversational Spanish from naturalisation applicants. Doctors and lawyers in the districts named here speak English.

What about schools later?Family

Buenos Aires has a deep set of bilingual and international schools, most with waiting lists that start at age two, and fees quoted in dollars. Public kindergartens are free and often good. Enrol early; a school record is also evidence of residence.

Can we bring a pet?Life

Yes. SENASA, the animal health agency, requires a veterinary health certificate issued shortly before travel, an up-to-date rabies vaccine and, for some origins, blood tests. Airlines have their own rules. Allow a month of preparation.

How do we move money in and out?Money

Currency controls on individuals ended in April 2025 and the official and informal rates are within a few percent. Bank transfers into an Argentine account work once you have one; before that, Western Union and cash. Declare cash above USD 10,000 at the border. Many rents and private school fees are still quoted in dollars.

What are the seasons?Life

Reversed from the north. Buenos Aires is humid, hot from December to February and mild in winter. Mendoza is dry and sunny with real winters. Córdoba is temperate. Patagonia is cold and windy with long summer days; Bariloche's ski season runs July to September.

How do we pick a lawyer?Law

Look for a firm that does migration and citizenship work daily, ask how many citizenship files it has lodged in court since July 2026, and insist on a fixed fee and a written opinion on Scenario A versus B. Walk away from anyone selling guarantees, timelines or "packages".

The eight decisions only you can make

1

Which city for the first ninety days?

Buenos Aires holds every office and hospital; the others hold the lifestyle. You can move later, but each move restarts the evidence ledger.

Recommended: Buenos Aires for the paperwork year, then reconsider. It shortens every queue that matters.

2

A prepaga first, or a pregnancy first?

Joining a plan before conception makes the whole pregnancy covered at the standard price. Arriving pregnant means self-pay or a loaded premium.

Recommended: plan first. The sequencing is worth several thousand dollars and removes the tail risk.

3

Which door at day 80?

The DNM extension keeps you clean but leaves you a visitor. Changing category starts the clock the citizenship rules may count.

Recommended: change category if the home-country certificates have arrived; otherwise extend once and file at day 150.

4

Which scenario to plan around?

Scenario A is faster but depends on the Supreme Court. Scenario B is slower but certain to be no worse than the decree.

Recommended: plan on B's timeline and B's no-exit rule; file early if A holds. You lose nothing if A wins.

5

Do both of you run the no-exit clock at once?

Under Scenario B two years without leaving is a real cost for a family with parents in two other countries.

Recommended: decide who naturalises first; the passport is worth more to the South African partner.

6

Where is the tax home from month twelve?

Argentina will claim you after a year. The US never lets go. South Africa can be left formally or drifted out of.

Recommended: a written plan from a cross-border adviser before month ten, not after.

7

Which entity, and where seated?

An SAS in the capital is fastest; an SRL is the form banks know; Córdoba's registry and talent pool suit an engineering team.

Recommended: SAS or SRL seated where you live, with the Knowledge Economy application filed in the first year.

8

Marry before, after, or not at all?

The child route does not need a marriage. A marriage adds a spouse-based residence option, simplifies inheritance and some tax questions, and adds one more apostilled document.

Recommended: decide on your own terms; if you marry, do it before the residence file so the certificate travels with the rest.
